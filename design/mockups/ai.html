1

What the model decides, and what it cannot

Fothering uses an AI model to name the cause of a spike and to write the email you read. It never touches your SES account directly: every closure it proposes passes fixed checks first, and only your reply reopens a lane.

2

The interlock between the model and SES

The model can ask for one tool, close a lane, and that tool requires an evidence record. Its handler recomputes the thresholds from the stored events and refuses if they do not hold, whatever the model wrote.

3

What the model does, and what it never does

The split is enforced by permissions and code, not by instructions in a prompt.

It does

  • Read the minimized event window for one lane and the lanes beside it.
  • Name a cause with the events that support it, or say the cause is unknown.
  • Propose closing one configuration set, with that evidence attached.
  • Write the email you receive, and read your reply to tell reopen from keep closed.

It never

  • Calls SES itself. Only the checked tool handler does, and only for sets you listed at deploy.
  • Pauses the whole account. That permission is not in the template.
  • Reopens a lane. Only your reply or your choice in the console does.
  • Sees a recipient address, a subject line or a message header.

5

Where it can be wrong

Naming a cause is a judgment, and a model can make a bad one.

It can pick the wrong tag when two causes overlap, such as an import batch sent with a new template. The fixed check limits the damage to one configuration set, and the email lists what was ruled out so you can see the reasoning.

It can say unknown when a person would have guessed right. That is the intended trade: a missed closure costs you a question, a wrong closure costs you a lane of mail.

It cannot see anything SES does not publish. A cause that only shows in your own application logs stays invisible to it.
