The same bounce spike, three responses

AWS already gives you two tools for this moment. Both are good at what they do. Neither tells you which stream caused the damage, and one of them closes by threshold alone.

Question Virtual Deliverability Manager advisorAmazon SES CloudWatch alarm plus Lambda pauseAWS documented recipe Fotheringthis project
Does it act? Recommends. You act. Pauses when the rate crosses your threshold. Pauses one configuration set once the evidence names a cause.
Does it say why? Names account-level issues such as a missing DMARC record. No. The alarm knows a rate, not a cause. Cites the batch, template, provider or record, or says undetermined.
Is the pause proven? Nothing is paused. Only if you build the check. Reads the setting back and records the answer.
How does it reopen? Not applicable. By hand, in the console or CLI. You reply to its email. SES receives the reply.

How one lane gets closed

The order is fixed, the way an isolation procedure is. No step is skipped, and a step that cannot finish stops the sequence and asks.

  1. 1

    Watch every event

    Sends, deliveries, bounces, complaints, rejects and delays arrive through EventBridge, grouped by configuration set and by your message tags.

  2. 2

    Name the cause, or say undetermined

    A spike is attributed to one import batch, template, receiving provider or missing authentication record, with the events that show it.

  3. 3

    Close one lane and verify

    Sending is switched off for that configuration set only, then read back through a second call before anything is reported.

  4. 4

    Ask you by email

    You get what it saw, what it closed and what stays open. Reply "reopen" or "keep closed", and it acts and confirms.

What it will not do

  • Close a lane on a guess. When the evidence does not settle the cause, nothing closes and you are asked.
  • Pause your whole account. The smallest closure that stops the damage is one configuration set.
  • Reopen on its own. Only your reply reopens a lane.
  • Keep recipient addresses. It stores the receiving domain and a keyed hash, in Frankfurt, for 30 days.
