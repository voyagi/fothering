2

How one lane gets closed

The order is fixed, the way an isolation procedure is. A step that cannot finish stops the sequence and asks you instead.

  1. 1

    Watch every event

    Sends, deliveries, bounces, complaints, rejects and delays arrive through EventBridge, grouped by configuration set and by your message tags.

  2. 2

    Name the cause, or say unknown

    A spike is traced to one import batch, template, receiving provider or missing authentication record, with the events that show it.

  3. 3

    Close one lane, then read it back

    Sending is switched off for that configuration set only, then confirmed through a second call, like the filled valve on partner-import in row 1.

  4. 4

    Ask you by email

    You get what it saw, what it closed and what stays open. Your reply reopens the lane or keeps it closed.

3

The email you get, and the reply that decides

Your inbox is the console you already watch. The message says what the tag in row 1 says, in full sentences, and waits for one word back.

From
Fothering, sent through your own verified SES identity
Subject
Closed: partner-import, one lane in eu-central-1

Fothering's AI agent wrote the paragraph headed "Why". Code decided the closure and wrote the rest from the closure record.

What Fothering saw. Hard bounces on the partner-import configuration set, every one tagged import_batch=2026-09-14-b, with 550 5.1.1 (no such mailbox) from several receiving providers. Receipts, password-resets and newsletter look normal.

What it did. Turned sending off for partner-import only at 14:03 UTC and read it back: SendingEnabled is false. SES now refuses sends that use this configuration set, so your importer will see errors on this lane until it reopens.

What stays open. Receipts, password-resets, newsletter.

Why. The bounces follow one import batch, not a template or a provider: the same template delivers on the other lanes, and the bounces come from several providers at once.

Reply reopen to resume sending, or keep closed to leave it paused.

4

The same bounce spike, three responses

AWS already gives you two tools for this moment, and both are good at their job. One advises and never acts. The other acts on a threshold without saying why.

Schedule of responses. Fothering column: this project, fixture build.
Question Virtual Deliverability Manager advisorAmazon SES CloudWatch alarm plus Lambda pauseAWS documented recipe Fotheringthis project
Does it act? Recommends. You act. Pauses when the rate crosses your threshold. Pauses one configuration set once the evidence names a cause.
Does it say why? Flags issues per sending identity: DKIM, SPF, DMARC, bounces, complaints, IP listings. No. The alarm knows a rate, not a cause. Cites the batch, template, provider or record, or says unknown.
Is the pause proven? Nothing is paused. Only if you build the check. Reads the setting back and records the answer.
How does it reopen? Not applicable. By hand, in the console or CLI. You reply to its email. SES receives the reply.
5

What it will not do

  • Close a lane on a guess. When the evidence does not settle the cause, nothing closes and you are asked.
  • Pause your whole account. The smallest closure that stops the damage is one configuration set.
  • Reopen on its own, or report a closure the read-back did not confirm. Only your reply reopens a lane.
  • Keep recipient addresses. It stores the receiving domain and a keyed hash in Frankfurt, deleted within a few days after 30 days.
